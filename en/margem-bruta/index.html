What Is Gross Margin?

Gross margin is the percentage of sales revenue left over after deducting only the Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) — that is, the direct cost of buying or producing the product sold. It does not account for operating expenses such as rent, salaries, marketing, or income taxes.

Gross Margin (%) = [(Sales Revenue − COGS) ÷ Sales Revenue] × 100

Example: a store brings in $50,000 in revenue for the month. The total cost of goods sold is $30,000.

  • Gross Profit = $50,000 − $30,000 = $20,000
  • Gross Margin = $20,000 ÷ $50,000 × 100 = 40%

This means 40% of everything the store brings in is left over to cover operating expenses and generate net profit.

What Is Gross Margin Used For?

Gross margin is the ideal indicator for:

  • Evaluating purchasing and production efficiency: a gross margin that falls month after month indicates product costs are rising faster than the selling price.
  • Comparing product categories: which lines have the highest margin? Where should sales efforts be focused?
  • Setting a feasible ceiling for operating expenses: if gross margin is 40%, the business can spend at most 40% of revenue on expenses without running a net loss.
  • Analyzing the impact of discounts: a 10% discount on a product with a 30% gross margin wipes out a third of the gross profit.

Gross Margin vs. Net Margin: What's the Difference?

Indicator What it deducts What it's used for
Gross Margin Only COGS (product cost) Purchasing and pricing efficiency
Operating Margin COGS + operating expenses Efficiency of the operation as a whole
Net Margin COGS + expenses + income taxes Real profitability of the business

Example — From revenue to net profit

Revenue: $100,000 | COGS: $55,000 | Operating expenses: $30,000 | Taxes: $6,000

  • Gross Margin = (100,000 − 55,000) ÷ 100,000 = 45%
  • Operating Margin = (45,000 − 30,000) ÷ 100,000 = 15%
  • Net Margin = (15,000 − 6,000) ÷ 100,000 = 9%
